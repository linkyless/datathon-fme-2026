# Decisions

> Logbook of the event. Module names are the current ones (during the event they were Spanish: `aprendiz` ->

> the team role: Operator, Ranking, Evaluation, Data. Rows are only ever appended.

Every non-trivial decision, in 2-3 sentences a product manager can follow. It's for the jury (30 points).

| Time | Who | Decision | Alternatives discarded | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sat 12:00 | team | A single AGENTS.md for OpenCode and Claude Code | One file per tool | So that the four agents work with the same rules |

| Sat 13:00 | team | Everything in Python first; C++/Rust only if a piece is slow | Starting in C++/Rust | Data libraries and agents work better in Python; 3 requests/s doesn't need more speed |
| Sat 13:00 | team | First the simple model (co-visits) and then, on top, the big one (rerank with a model) | Going straight to the big model | The big one needs the simple one's candidates and the same evaluator: nothing gets thrown away |
| Sat 13:45 | team | Strategy from docs/research.md: session candidates (revisits + type- and recency-weighted co-visits + subcategory/brand), an evaluator calibrated against the leaderboard, and only then logistic regression -> LightGBM and Thompson over a few variants | Going straight to gradient boosting or neural networks | Simple neighbour methods compete on short sessions (Ludewig/Jannach 2020, OTTO); the teams that went straight to GBM are below the baseline |
| Sat 14:08 | Operator | Handover: the starter baseline stops and src/service.py goes in with e-samen-v0, without waiting for the offline measurement | Waiting for Evaluation's evaluator | It's practice (doesn't score), the idea was already proven live by another team (+20 %) and this way we measure with real shoppers; rolling back takes 10 s |
| Sat 15:55 | Data | Tukey panel (`src/panel_tukey.py` -> `data/panel.html`): 9 panels, one per question of the challenge, each ending in the decision it feeds (move up/roll back a version, robustness, ranker features, cold priority, trends and catalog_updates cadence) | Power BI / Shiny / one metrics panel per role | A single dashboard with the SAME definitions as the evaluator and the service (purchase = purchased != '', ts in UTC) avoids two truths; it's self-contained with no dependencies: shown as a demo in the presentation and it's our Tukey side challenge |
| Sat 15:30 | Ranking | Co-visits WITHOUT dividing by popularity (exponent 0) and, from R3, only revisits (0.3) as a candidate; subcategory/brand affinity and price off | The deep research's exponent 0.5 and turning on all the R3 ideas | On 3,530 real carousel purchases after 7/11, the bought product lands in our 10 in 14.8 % of cases with exponent 0 vs 11.5 % with 0.5; revisits take it to 15.8 %. Subcategory, brand and price don't improve: co-visits already bring similar products. A rough Ranking check, pending Evaluation's official measurement |
| Sat 16:15 | Ranking | CORRECTS the 15:30 row: there's no proof that co-visits beat v0. Decide with a live A/B | Trusting the 15:30 check | The "v0" in that check had the neighbours turned on by mistake (the bug Evaluation spotted). The real v0 gets 31.7 % of carousel purchases into our 10 vs 14.8 % for co-visits, but that history rewards looking like the old carousel (5 of its 10 slots were category best sellers), so it can't be decided offline |
| Sat 17:10 | Ranking | R4 trends: each product carries a "lift" (last half hour's activity / its normal pace over the previous 3 h) and the score is multiplied by lift^eta; off until tested in an A/B | Last hour's best sellers without comparing to normal (it'd always be the usual ones) | The final will have new trends and the baseline doesn't see them. Comparing with each product's normal pace detects what's RISING, not what always sells. Measured live: what rises in one half hour stays 1.14x above normal in the next, so the signal lasts long enough to use |
| Sat 17:15 | Evaluation | Every offline measurement comes with a 95 % confidence interval (paired bootstrap, 200 resamples, fixed seed) of the difference in hit_rate and purchase_hit vs v0, the starter and the current version | Comparing means only | With ~3,700 clicks and ~1,400 purchases in the test set, a small difference can be luck. The interval says whether it is: if it doesn't include 0, the improvement or drop is real. "Paired" = both versions are judged on the same drawn visits |
| Sat 17:40 | Ranking | R5: a logistic regression learns how much each signal is worth (popularity, same category, already bought, co-visit, price...) instead of adding points by eye; weighted by 1/propensity and with a slot signal | Going straight to LightGBM or keep tuning weights by hand | It's a single neuron: every coefficient can be shown to the jury ("having bought it before multiplies the purchase odds by 1.83"). It doesn't use product_id, so it works for new products. Reordering what was shown, it ranks the bought product 1st 48.5 % of the time vs 40.9 % for the category best sellers |
| Sat 16:45 | Operator | Test ideas with live A/B tests (fixed split per shopper) instead of offline only | Trusting offline | Offline can only reward what the old carousel showed: 100 % of the test purchases are in `shown`, so it rewards looking like the old system |
| Sat 17:35 | Operator | Live: CF (v0 without what the shopper already bought) at 75 % and C (v0) at 25 % as control. Co-visits, revisits, trends and R5 without the filter, out | Keep 4 arms; R5 as is | The organisers say there are no repurchases live; in the history there are (62 %), which is why R5 and offline said the opposite. CF is v0 with a single change |
| Sat 17:45 | Operator | **Process fix**: this afternoon 5 changes went live without an Evaluation measurement. From now on nothing goes live without Evaluation and every finding is written in the repo first | Keep testing live directly | We were going faster than the team: the others (and their agents) didn't know what was running or why |
| Sat 18:05 | team | Next model: R6 = LightGBM (tree) with the shopper profile and relative price, trained without repurchases; measured by Evaluation before going live | Keep hand-made rules; R5 as is | 97.6 % of purchases are in the category of the last viewed product, so what decides is WHICH product within it: people buy at half the price of what they viewed, often from the same subcategory/brand. A tree learns those combinations; a regression adds them up separately |
| Sat 19:30 | Ranking | R6: LightGBM trained as a choice within the carousel (each visit with a purchase is a group of 10 and the bought one wins), without repurchases and with the Data role's profiles; off (`use_tree`) until Evaluation measures it | Stay with v2 only (conditional logit) | Trees learn combinations a single neuron can't. On the same test purchases as v2, it ranks the bought product 1st 38.4 % of the time vs 34.9 % for v2. Lesson: product quality has to be computed "out of fold"; otherwise the tree memorises the answer |
| Sat 19:07 | Operator | A7 at 100 %: the tree for whoever has viewed something; best sellers (without bought products) for whoever hasn't | A6 (with the favourite for cold shoppers), v4 (live logistic) | Measured live: the tree raises conversion +32 % and revenue +43 % on non-cold requests; the favourite hurt cold shoppers because we measured it with future leakage. Lesson for the jury: "today" signals must be computed ONLY with what came before each answer |
| Sat 20:30 | Evaluation | New model `src/learner.py`: the weights are learned ONLY from our live feedback, with few signals (cart today, old cart not bought, viewed in the session, the shopper's affinity with the category and the subcategory, and two numbers per product). It doesn't go live until measured in an A/B against C | Keep refining models trained on the old carousel (R5, v2, R6 tree); a big model with many signals | The old carousel only showed popular products, so no model trained on it can learn that a product in the cart sells 10 times more than an unrelated one (2.5 % vs 0.22 % in the first slot). Our feedback does show it. With 11 signals every weight can be explained in one sentence, it works the same for new products and shoppers, and a tree with the same signals doesn't predict better |
| Sat 21:35 | Evaluation | Exploration: in half of the learner's answers, the last two slots (9 and 10) show a new product and one the model wouldn't have picked, always from a category that shopper cares about. The half is picked by request id, so the other half works as the comparison without setting up another arm | Exploring with random products from the whole shop; exploring in the top slots; not exploring | We only learn from what we show, and the model shows what it already believes is good: of new products we had shown 1,698 and seen 4 purchases all Saturday, and in the final almost everything will be new. The last slots are worth a third of the first, so exploring there is cheap (about 0.04 purchases per 100 requests according to the model) |
| Sun 00:15 | Evaluation | AP7: add the "same subcategory as the last viewed product" signal to the learner and test it with two strengths: the one learned from our feedback (x1.39) and a forced one (x3) | Trusting only the history recall (it would ask for x10 or more); trusting only the feedback (change nothing); embedding neighbours and a co-purchase table | In the history purchases, the best sellers of the subcategory the shopper is looking at hit 20 % of the time, twice any other source; but in our carousel those products only sell 40 % more than other subcategories. The two measurements disagree, so it's tested live: if the strong version wins, the history recall is useful for deciding; if not, the feedback rules |
| Sun 06:05 | Evaluation | AP8: the model learns "out of the 10 products we showed, which one the shopper chose" instead of "is this product bought, yes or no?". It also uses personal taste from the history (factorisation), the device and whether the product is new. It goes to a 50/50 A/B against the control, capped at 2 new products unrelated to the shopper per list | Keep the per-product model and add signals; two models, one for new shoppers and one for known ones; trees (LightGBM) on top | Someone with something in the cart buys more whatever you show them. The previous model credited that to the cart product and gave "their own stuff" too much weight. Comparing only within each list cancels that effect and what's left is what helps ordering: on unseen data it predicts better which one gets chosen (choice measure 1.81 -> 1.64; random 2.30). Trees improved a little more, but what they found was the shape of two signals, which fits in the simple model we can explain weight by weight. The cap on new products is caution: the model believes they're worth almost 5x more, but we had only shown them half a time per list |
| Sun 07:50 | Evaluation | Close the project with AP7 (`aphc` weights) as the final version and clean up the repo: code in English, the service with the learner only (every version still runs with `--arms`), Saturday's code kept in `src/` and in `version/*` tags, and the event's organisation in `docs/archive/` | Keep AP8 (tie live, better offline); delete the old versions | AP8 didn't prove it sells more than AP7 in the A/B (1.69 vs 1.77, within noise) and loses on new shoppers, which is what the final brings most. AP7 has the most hours measured live. Everything else is kept because the mistakes (history repurchases, future leakage, short A/Bs) are part of the story |
